## 1. The wedge (verified — 3 sentences)

Serving-layer depth now exists in print — Kiely/Baseten and Wang & Hu/O'Reilly both shipped in 2026 — but both are written for engineers who run engines (CUDA-to-Kubernetes operators, hosting teams), not for harness engineers who consume inference through provider APIs. No book-length title takes the harness/agent engineer as its primary reader and teaches the engine room from the client side of the contract: latency and cost arithmetic, cache-friendly loop design, rate limits as physics, routing and budgets. That seam — Vol. I's "Agent = Model + Harness" continued downward through the API boundary — is the wedge, though the bare title "Inference Engineering" now collides with Baseten and must be differentiated by subtitle and framing. [books-positioning-wedge.md]

**Title caveat:** Baseten's free 2026 book plus its interactive companion (inferenceengineering.tech) will dominate search for the bare phrase "Inference Engineering" — win discovery via subtitle/series keywords (harness, agents, TTFT, provider APIs) and position vs Kiely explicitly in the preface as complementary, citable, not substitutable: his book is for people who run engines; ours is for people whose loops live on those engines. [books-positioning-wedge.md]

## 3. The free-alternatives answer: what vLLM docs/blogs cannot give

- **Docs are reference-shaped, not narrative.** vLLM's docs (V1 architecture, quantization matrix across FP8/MXFP4/NVFP4/INT8/GPTQ/AWQ/GGUF, -O0…-O3 tuning levels) tell you *what knobs exist*; they never narrate *why a design won* or walk the arithmetic of a sizing decision. SGLang's docs are strong on spec-decode parameters but even third parties note gaps vs vLLM's accumulated tutorials; the real depth sits in LMSYS blog posts. [books-free-alternatives.md]
- **Blogs are per-technique, vendor-tied, and perishable.** Baseten's GLM-5/GLM-5.2 writeups, Fireworks' Blackwell kernel work, and NVIDIA's TRT-LLM series are individually excellent and collectively scattered, GPU/model-specific, marketing-adjacent — numbers expire with each hardware generation. [books-free-alternatives.md]
- **The corpus itself admits the gap.** The open-source *llm-inference-at-scale* handbook: the knowledge "is scattered across research papers, blog posts, source code comments, and tribal knowledge. No single resource connected the full picture." [books-free-alternatives.md]
- **What only this book gives:** (1) a curated end-to-end path across the whole stack — the only free one is Baseten's vendor book, written for engine operators; (2) worked arithmetic in one consistent voice — KV-cache bytes, FLOPs, rooflines, TTFT/TPOT math, 429/backoff budgets — instead of one-off lecture slides; (3) a stable, vendor-neutral narrative with dated-snapshot traced numbers that survives docs churn and the benchmark treadmill; (4) the harness↔serving seam as through-line, which no free source assembles. [books-free-alternatives.md, books-positioning-wedge.md]

## 4. Objection handling

**"Huyen's *AI Engineering* already covers inference."** It gives inference one chapter of ten — ch9, pp. 405–447, roughly 42 of 534 pages — and her own chapter summary concedes most app developers "will use these APIs with their built-in optimization," so techniques stay at the conceptual/mechanism survey level: no engine internals, no sizing arithmetic, no agent-era serving (the TOC predates MCP and the 2025 agent-framework wave; agents get ~30 pp inside a combined chapter). This book is the volume her ch9 points toward but doesn't write: the engine room taught book-length, from the client side of the API contract, for engineers whose loops pay the TTFT and cache bills. Complementary, not substitutable — we reference her definitive eval chapters rather than duplicate them, and take serving depth "orders of magnitude" beyond survey scope. [books-ai-engineering-huyen.md]

**"Just read the vLLM docs and the provider blogs."** Docs tell you what knobs exist, not why a design won or how to size a decision — they're reference material, not a curriculum; the deep blog content is per-technique, GPU/model-specific, vendor-adjacent, and expires with each generation (Aug 2026's SGLang posts supersede 2025's; Blackwell numbers supersede Hopper). Assembling the full picture from docs, papers, source comments, and tribal knowledge is precisely the job nobody has time for — the open-source handbook says no single resource connects it. The book is the map: a curated path, worked arithmetic in one voice, dated-snapshot numbers you can cite, vendor-neutral where Baseten's free alternative is vendor-authored and engine-operator-framed. You'll still read the docs daily — with the model of why they're shaped that way. [books-free-alternatives.md]

**"I'll wait for the video courses."** They exist and they're good: Stanford CS336 Lecture 10 (free, with transcript and runnable code), DeepLearning.AI's ~1h38m vLLM short course, GPU MODE's 106 free lectures. But each covers fundamentals or a single kernel; none assembles the harness↔serving seam end-to-end, and none is referenceable at 2 a.m. when you need the KV-bytes formula or a 429-backoff budget for a design review. Video also dates at lecture speed — mid-2026 techniques (Blackwell spec decode, PD-disaggregation economics, NVFP4) reach blogs first and course playlists a year later. A book with dated-snapshot boxes plus a buildable tinyengine companion is the durable, executable layer under the churn — you can't run a lecture. [books-free-alternatives.md]

## 5. Why now (as of mid-2026)

- **The engine-side shelf just closed.** Baseten's free *Inference Engineering* and O'Reilly's *Hands-On LLM Serving* (Apr 2026) shipped within months of each other — topic white space is gone; only the audience seam (harness/agent engineers) is open, and nobody has claimed it yet. [books-positioning-wedge.md]
- **The reader finally has a name.** "Harness engineering" became industry vocabulary by mid-2026 — Fowler, OpenAI, Anthropic's "effective harnesses," LangChain's "Agent = Model + Harness" — so buyers self-identify; no serving book speaks to them. [books-positioning-wedge.md]
- **The agent shelf is crowded but hollow at the bottom.** 2025–26 agent titles from Manning (Feb and Jun 2026), Packt (2025), and O'Reilly (Sep 2025, Mar 2026) all treat serving as a black box; serving coverage beneath agents exists only in uneven self-published vLLM titles. [books-agents-2025-2026.md]
- **Cite-freshness window is open.** Most competitors predate mid-2026 hardware framing (B200/GH200/Blackwell); only Wang & Hu and one KDP listing claim current-GPU coverage — a book shipping now with traced, dated snapshot numbers is the current-generation reference. [books-llm-inference-titles.md]
- **Free content is churning faster than it consolidates.** Aug 2026 alone brought SGLang weight-cache-daemon recovery and Blackwell batch-1 spec decode; vendor benchmarks expire per GPU generation; the neutral, durable, full-picture treatment still doesn't exist anywhere. [books-free-alternatives.md]
