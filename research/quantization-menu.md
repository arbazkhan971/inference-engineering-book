# The quantization menu: FP8, INT8, INT4 weight-only, and KV-cache quant in serving
researched: 2026-08-27 · researcher: glm-5.3-flash

## Key facts
- SmoothQuant enables W8A8 INT8 (weights *and* activations) with no retraining, achieving up to **1.56x speedup and 2x memory reduction** vs FP16 on OPT-175B-class models (arXiv:2211.10438, 2023; confirmed on mit-han-lab GitHub, fetched 2026-08-27). Note: the correct arXiv ID is 2211.10438, not 2211.03888 — a commonly mistyped citation.
- GPTQ (arXiv:2210.17323, ICLR 2023) quantizes GPT/OPT-family models to **3–4 bits per weight with negligible perplexity loss**, e.g. 4-bit GPTQ matches FP16 far better than 4-bit RTN on WikiText-2 perplexity (per-model tables in IST-DASLab/gptq README, fetched 2026-08-27). The paper reports end-to-end inference speedups over FP16 of **~3.25x on NVIDIA A100 and ~4.5x on A6000** in its extreme-quantization regime (arXiv:2210.17323v2, 2022).
- AWQ (arXiv:2306.00978, MLSys 2024) is weight-only INT3/INT4 quantization that protects the **~1% of salient channels** identified from activation statistics, avoiding the mixed-precision fallback GPTQ-style methods need; its inference engine delivers **>3x speedup over FP16 HuggingFace** on desktop and mobile GPUs, including running Llama-2-70B on a Jetson Orin 64GB (hanlab.mit.edu/projects/awq, fetched 2026-08-27). TinyChat 2.0 is a further 1.5–1.7x over the prior engine (same source, Dec 2024).
- FP8 KV cache in vLLM: `--kv-cache-dtype fp8` runs attention QK and ScoreV matmuls in FP8 e4m3. April 2026 vLLM stress tests (H100, concurrency 8) show Llama-3.1-8B at **14.9% higher output throughput (450.3 → 517.5 tok/s)** and gpt-oss-20b at 4.8% (831.6 → 871.8 tok/s) (vLLM project blog, 2026-04-22).
- FP8 KV accuracy: a Hopper FlashAttention-3 accumulation bug had dropped 128k needle-in-a-haystack from 91% (BF16) to 13% (FP8); the fix restored 89%. Reasoning benchmarks degrade at most 1–2 points (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking lowest recovery 97% on GPQA:Diamond); long-context MRCR recovers 93–98% of BF16 AUC up to 256k (vLLM project blog, 2026-04-22).
- vLLM's KV cache dtype menu has grown to per-scheme options: `fp8` (per-tensor scales), `fp8_per_token_head` / `int8_per_token_head` / `int4_per_token_head`, `nvfp4`, and `turboquant_*` variants (vllm/config/cache.py, fetched 2026-08-27; docs.vllm.ai Quantized KV Cache page, fetched 2026-08-27).
- gpt-oss models ship **MXFP4-quantized** (4-bit floating point with blockwise scaling) as their native format: gpt-oss-120b (117B total / 5.1B active params) fits a single 80GB H100/MI300X; gpt-oss-20b (21B total / 3.6B active) runs in ~16GB (openai/gpt-oss GitHub and openai.com, fetched 2026-08-27; model card arXiv:2508.10925, 2025). The quantization was applied via post-training so 4-bit is the intended deployment precision, not a degraded copy.
- Per-token decode cost (ITL slope vs context length) under FP8 KV cache on H100 is **54% of BF16** for Llama-3.1-8B, with break-even at ~7k tokens; below ~7k context FP8 KV is net-negative, so short-context agents should not enable it (vLLM project blog, 2026-04-22).

## How it works
- **Per-channel/per-group quant math.** Quantization maps FP16/BF16 values onto a smaller grid. For affine quantization per channel: `q = round(w / s) + z`, `w ≈ s · (q − z)`, where the scale `s = (max(w) − min(w)) / (2^b − 1)` for b-bit integers and `z` is a zero point. Weights are stored as `q` (b-bit ints) plus one scale per channel (or per group, e.g. g128 = one scale per 128 weights), and the scale is folded into the dequantization at load or fused into the GEMM. Worked example: a weight channel spanning [−0.08, +0.08] in INT4 (b=4, 15 levels) gets s = 0.16/15 ≈ 0.0107, so rounding error per weight is at most s/2 ≈ 0.0053 — finer-grained grouping shrinks each channel's range and thus the error.
- **W8A8 / FP8 (SmoothQuant lineage).** Activations have outlier channels that break naive INT8 quantization. SmoothQuant migrates the difficulty from activations to weights with a per-channel smoothing factor `s = max(|X|)^α`, dividing activations by s before the matmul and multiplying weights by s — mathematically identical output, but now both sides quantize cleanly to 8 bits. This is the ancestor of today's default FP8 serving on H100+/Hopper+ hardware, where e4m3/e5m2 floating point replaces INT8.
- **Weight-only INT4 (GPTQ/AWQ).** Only weights go to 4 bits; activations stay 16-bit. GPTQ uses second-order (approximate-Hessian) information to sequentially update not-yet-quantized weights to compensate each rounding error. AWQ instead observes that quality is protected by scaling salient channels (found from ~512 calibration samples) so that the 1% critical weights fall in representable range — no per-weight mixed precision needed. Because decode is memory-bandwidth-bound, halving/quarting weight bytes raises tokens/s even with integer→float dequant fused in the kernel.
- **KV-cache quant.** The KV cache grows linearly with tokens × layers × 2 × head_dim × dtype_bytes. FP8 halves those bytes, letting the same GPU hold roughly 2x the cached tokens (more concurrent long-context sessions before preemption). vLLM applies it per-tensor (one scale for Q, K, V each) or per-attention-head (`q_scale = [num_heads]`), and runs the attention matmuls themselves in FP8 on tensor cores (docs.vllm.ai, fetched 2026-08-27).
- **Providers ship quant variants.** A model list like gpt-oss-120b vs 20b, or "fast/mini/lite" tiers, is often a quantization + sparsity (MoE) choice wearing a different name. MXFP4 with blockwise scaling (a shared exponent per block of values) is how a 117B model becomes an 80GB-GPU resident (openai/gpt-oss, fetched 2026-08-27). Reading a variant list: smaller/faster tiers ≈ lower-bit weights + fewer active params → lower $/Mtok and lower TPOT, at some quality cost the provider has baked in.

## Harness angle
When your harness picks between a provider's "standard" and "fast/lite" tier for an agent, you are choosing a point on the quantization tradeoff curve — so gate the choice on your own eval: long-context retrieval (>~7k-token histories) suffers disproportionately from KV-cache quantization (13% → 89% haystack swings from a single kernel bug; break-even only after ~7k tokens), while tool-calling short prompts tolerate INT4-class weight quant with ~97–99% benchmark recovery. Route cheap high-volume steps (classification, routing) to quantized tiers and keep long-context synthesis on full precision.
